Build your Weekly Briefing Agent

An agent that reads a week of email and calendar, works out what actually needs you, and writes the replies that matter — as one page you'd be happy to look at. You configure it in plain English. No code.

⏱ 60–90 min end to end 💻 Claude Code + a Claude Pro/Max plan 📁 github.com/nenti/weekly-briefing-agent
Tick the steps as you go — your progress stays in this browser. If you only get through step 6, you still have a working agent. Everything after that is upgrade.
BEFORE YOU START

Get the pieces in place

10 min

Three things, all free or already yours:

  • A Claude Pro or Max plan — this is what powers the agent.
  • Claude Code installed and signed in — install guide. The desktop app is the gentlest way in; the terminal works just as well.
  • A free GitHub account, so you can copy the starter project.

optional A throwaway Google account, for later when you point the agent at a real inbox and would rather not use your own.

STEP 1

Write your "About me"

10 min

An agent is only as good as what it knows about you. Rather than filling in a blank file, let Claude interview you. Open about-me-prompt.md, copy the prompt, paste it into Claude, and answer the six questions properly — including the ones about what drives you and how you like to sound.

Be specific where it counts. "Short and direct, never bullet points, always a full paragraph, I write in English unless the other person doesn't" is worth ten times more than "professional". The detail is what makes the drafts sound like you instead of like an assistant.

Save what it writes — it goes into me.md in the next step. Keep a copy somewhere: this profile is reusable in any AI tool.

This is context, not memory. Context is who you are and barely changes. Memory is what the agent keeps as you work together — last week's call, why a draft missed, what a customer prefers. Give it a notes/ folder and tell it to write there, and it gets better every week. If you already keep notes in Obsidian, that folder is your agent's memory.

STEP 2

Open the project

5 min

Copy the starter project to your machine and open that folder in Claude Code. You don't have to know any git — just ask:

SAY THIS

Clone github.com/nenti/weekly-briefing-agent into a new folder and open it.

Then look at what you've got. data/inbox.md is a week of emails, data/calendar.md is the week ahead. That's your agent's world for now — no login, nothing connected, nothing at risk.

STEP 3

Tell it who you are

5 min

Paste your About me into me.md, replacing the example. Then open rubric.md and make it yours: what must always reach you, what usually needs you, and what's safe to skip.

This is the file that makes your agent different from everyone else's. A recruiter's rubric, a founder's and a marketer's read nothing alike — and that's the whole point.
STEP 4

Build the agent itself

10 min

Now the part that matters. Open build-your-agent-prompt.md and paste the prompt. It asks you six questions — what the agent should do, how strict it should be, whether it may ever send anything on your behalf — and writes IDENTITY.md from your answers.

Nobody handed you that file. You wrote it, by answering questions — and that's the skill you actually came for.

Why it's called IDENTITY.md, not CLAUDE.md

IDENTITY.md is plain English with no tool named anywhere in it. Take that file to Cursor, to Codex, to whatever comes next — it still describes your agent. CLAUDE.md is three lines that point Claude Code at it: just the door, not the room.

STEP 5

Run it

5 min
SAY THIS

Read my inbox and calendar, apply my rubric, and build my weekly briefing.

Watch what it does: reads both files, sorts what needs you from what doesn't, drafts the replies, spots the clash in your week. Then open briefing.html.

If it looks plain, that's expected at first. The agent has example/briefing-example.html to aim at — if your result falls short of it, say so: "look at the example again and match that standard."
STEP 6

Tune it until you'd actually use it

10 min

This is where an agent stops being a demo. Disagree with a call it made? Don't fix the output — fix the rubric and run it again. That loop is the whole craft.

Things worth asking for, all of which came out of building this one:

  • "Make it a proper executive briefing — a photo at the top so I start the day inspired."
  • "Add the news that actually matters to me today, with a source link."
  • "Add the next public holidays using openholidaysapi.org."
  • "Be stricter — I only want things that genuinely need me today."
The holidays one is worth doing once: it's a public API, no key, no signup — and you never have to read a line of it. You say what you want; the agent deals with the format.
From here on it's upgrade — you already have a working agent
STEP 7

Point it at your real week

10 min

Go to claude.ai → Settings → Connectors and connect Gmail and Google Calendar. One click each, in the browser — no developer account, no API key. Claude Code picks them up on its own; check with /mcp.

Then change one line in IDENTITY.md: instead of the two sample files, read "my last 7 days of Gmail and this week's Google Calendar". Nothing else changes. Run it again.

Two things that will trip you up. Connectors only load when you're signed in with your Claude account — if an ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is set in your environment they silently don't appear (/status tells you). And a brand-new throwaway inbox is empty: the connector can read and draft, but it can't invent incoming mail. Send it a handful of messages first, or you're testing against nothing.
STEP 8

Let it run without you

15 min

A briefing you have to ask for isn't much of an upgrade. Two ways to make it turn up on its own:

On your laptop

Ask Claude Code to /schedule a run every weekday morning. Simple, and it runs while your machine is on.

In the cloud

A routine runs in Anthropic's cloud on its own schedule — laptop shut, nothing running locally. It gets its own checkout of your project, so push it to GitHub first.

The catch worth knowing up front: a cloud routine only has the connectors you've set up at claude.ai/customize/connectors — what you connected locally does not travel with it, and neither do local files outside the repo. Have your agent state in the briefing whether it read real data or the samples, so a sample run can never quietly pass itself off as your real week.
STEP 9

Point the same pattern somewhere else

open-ended

Everything you just built is three moves: read a source → judge it against a rubric you wrote → write where you already look. Change what it reads and what it judges by, and the same skeleton does a different job:

  • Screener — a list of companies in, scored against your criteria, ranked.
  • Radar — a handful of competitor pages, diffed weekly, only what changed.
  • Meeting notes — a transcript in, decisions and owners out.
  • Content repurposer — one long piece in, five posts out, in your voice.
