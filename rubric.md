# What matters to me

*This is the heart of your agent. It's how it decides what needs you and what doesn't.
Write it the way you'd brief a smart new assistant on their first day. Plain English.*

## Always put in front of me

- Anything from a **customer or prospect** — especially a question, a complaint, or a
  buying signal.
- Anyone **blocked waiting on my decision**.
- Money: **invoices, contracts, anything with a deadline**.
- A **person** reaching out directly (not a tool, not a list).

## Usually needs me, but not urgent

- My team asking for input or review.
- A meeting that needs prep before it happens.
- An intro or a warm relationship worth keeping alive.

## Safe to skip

- Newsletters, product announcements, "we've updated our terms".
- Automated notifications and receipts I don't need to act on.
- Anything where I'm only CC'd and no one asked me anything.

## How I like my briefing

- **Strict.** I'd rather see 4 things that truly need me than 20 "maybes".
- For each reply, give me a **first sentence** so I can just keep going.
- Tell me the **one decision** that will unblock the most people first.

> Change every line above to fit your world. A recruiter's rubric, a founder's rubric,
> and an HR lead's rubric all look different — that difference is your agent.
